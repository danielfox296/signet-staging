Funerals and memorials

A musician for a funeral, a memorial service or a celebration of life.

As people find their seats, a guitarist is playing the hymn the family chose, and later in the service, the one song that was theirs. A funeral or a memorial has three or four places where music goes, from the prelude to the reception afterward. One guitarist or a pianist covers the service, upright bass and guitar cover the reception, and a service a few days away is a normal request.

It’s one of our daytime bookings, arranged with whoever is leading the service, the family, the funeral director, the officiant or the church, with one personal contact from the first email to the last song.

The service

Where the music goes in a funeral or a memorial.

The order is the officiant’s or the family’s. These are the four places a musician usually plays, and any one of them can be booked on its own.

As people arrive
Twenty to thirty minutes of prelude while people find a seat, played by one guitarist or a pianist: hymns, the standards, and the songs the family names. It’s the easiest part to add, and it means the service begins in music.
During the service
One song or two, at the moment the family or the officiant chooses. It’s usually the song that was theirs, played on guitar or piano or sung, and we agree the timing with the officiant beforehand.
The recessional and the graveside
Music as everyone stands and leaves, and at the graveside if the family wants it there. The same guitarist who played the chapel plays the graveside, without power, on a battery-powered system or none at all.
The reception or the celebration of life
Music under the conversation afterward, at the church hall, a restaurant or the house. A duo can play the standards, or a singer with a guitar can play the songs the person loved. If the family would like a jazz band, our jazz duo and trio page has the options.

Who plays it

Guitar, piano, strings, or a voice.

Acoustic or Spanish guitar

The solo guitarist plays hymns, standards and the songs the family names on steel strings, and the Spanish guitarist plays the same service on nylon strings, which is the sound in the clip above. Either one plays a small chapel without amplification and moves to the graveside.

Piano

Where the chapel, the church or the funeral home has a piano, a pianist plays the prelude, the song and the recessional on it, and sings when the family wants the words.

A singer

The singer and guitarist is the booking when the words matter: a hymn sung, a country song, the one from the year they married. One voice and a guitar, and the same musician plays the prelude instrumentally.

When the family wants strings, a violinist, or violin and cello, plays Ave Maria and the hymns in the chapel and at the graveside. For the reception, the jazz duo or the trio plays the hour after. When a celebration of life at a home or a club runs into the evening, the private parties page covers a longer booking.

Booking it

Planned with the officiant, on short notice.

Send the date, the venue and the time of the service as soon as you have them, even if the service is this week. The musician and the officiant or the funeral director then agree the order of service and the cues, so on the day nobody has to signal anything from a pew.

The songs are yours to choose. Hymns, the standards, a folk song, the song from a first dance forty years ago: tell us the titles and the musician learns what isn’t already in the set. Where a family isn’t sure, the musician suggests from what’s usually asked for, and the song list is on the repertoire page.

Questions

Asked about music for a funeral or a memorial.

Can we hire a musician for a funeral on short notice?
Yes. A funeral is usually arranged inside a week, and a date a few days away is a normal request. Send the date, the venue and the time of the service, and we'll come back with a recommendation and a price. Signet Artists books funeral and memorial musicians across Denver and the Front Range.
Can a musician play at the graveside?
Yes. A guitar plays a graveside or a scattering without power, on a battery-powered system or none at all, and the same guitarist can play the service before it. Cemeteries have their own rules about where a musician stands and whether amplification is allowed, and we ask them beforehand.
What does the church or funeral home need to do?
Very little. Churches, funeral homes and cemeteries each have their own rules about amplification and where a musician can stand, and we ask them before the day. Sound support comes with the booking where the venue has none, and we can send a certificate of insurance to any venue that asks.
Can the service and the reception be one booking?
Yes. A service and its reception together are one booking, and if there's a song the family wants, let us know and we can usually learn it.
