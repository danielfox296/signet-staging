Live music ideas

What music works for your market, library or rec center?

Farmers markets
A singer with a guitar, a solo guitarist or an acoustic duo is a great fit for a farmers market, and they can play in sets across the morning. They’ll play folk, country and singer-songwriter songs that people know the words to. Planning music for a whole season of Saturdays? You can send us all the dates at once.
Libraries
A library is built for quiet, so we usually book a solo musician or a duo. We can help with an adult program, a summer concert on the lawn, an afternoon of standards for older adults or a sound bath for a wellness program. René Moffatt is a featured artist in the Denver Public Library’s Volume Denver program, and Your Bird Can Sing plays the Beatles for all ages.
Rec centers and community centers
Community and rec centers book music for all kinds of events. The Americana band is a great choice for an outdoor summer evening, as a trio or a quartet. For an indoor event, like a holiday open house or a lunch for older adults, a solo musician or a duo is usually all you need.
Summer concert series
Many summer concert series put out a call for performers. For a park stage, we’d suggest the Americana band, Tejas Singh, Tony Medina, René Moffatt with his band or the yacht rock band. Send us the call and we’ll suggest the act that fits the stage.

What program coordinators ask us

Need stage sizes and power details for your facilities office? Check out our technical page. Planning a program in a resort town? Learn more on our mountain corridor page.

How loud is it?
At a market, we’ll keep it loud enough to hear from the next stall and quiet enough that vendors can still talk with their customers. In a library, we’ll keep it about as loud as a conversation, and a guitarist in a meeting space can play with no amplification at all. On a park stage, a band can turn it up as far as your permit allows.
What do you need outdoors?
You won’t need much for a guitarist or a singer with a guitar, since they can play on a battery-powered sound system or with no amplification. A band with drums usually needs its own power source, and if a park stage already has a sound system, we’ll want to talk with whoever runs it beforehand. Whatever the size of the act, we’ll need a covered, level, dry spot, and we’ll work out a rain plan with you ahead of time. A park or a plaza sometimes needs a permit for amplified sound, and the market or the city usually holds it. If amplified sound isn’t allowed, a solo musician or a small acoustic group can play without it.
How much space do the musicians need?
A solo musician needs about 6 by 4 feet, a duo about 8 by 6 and a trio about 12 by 8, on the ground or on a stage. Setup usually takes about 45 minutes for a solo musician and an hour for a duo or a trio, and we’ll get that done before your program starts.
Do you work with purchase orders and city vendor paperwork?
Yes, we do, and we can name the city, the district or the library as additional insured. Just let us know what the business office needs.
