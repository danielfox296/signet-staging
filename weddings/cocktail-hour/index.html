After the ceremony, before dinner

Live music for cocktail hour at a Colorado wedding.

Right after the ceremony, the two of you head off for photos and your guests have about an hour to fill on their own. Live music can turn that hour into the start of the party. We often recommend a jazz duo, because your guests can easily talk over it and still enjoy the sound of an upright bass up close.

Our duo plays jazz standards (classic songs like Autumn Leaves) on upright bass and guitar. An acoustic duo or a solo guitarist can be a great choice, too, if you’d like a different flavor. Song list

What music should play during cocktail hour?

We’d suggest jazz standards or singer-songwriter covers, the kind of songs your guests will recognize even if they can’t name them.

The volume. The duo plays at about the level of the conversation around it, so nobody has to lean in to be heard. That makes it easier for people to get to know each other, since many of your guests may be meeting for the first time.

The songs. Jazz standards sound sophisticated, and your guests can enjoy them without having to stop and listen. Thinking about a trio, or want to see more of the song list? Learn more about our jazz duo and trio.

Outdoors

What an outdoor cocktail hour sounds like.

Many Colorado cocktail hours happen outdoors, and music sounds different in the open air than it does in a ballroom. There are no walls for the sound to bounce off, so your guests only hear what’s coming straight from the musicians. That’s why the number of musicians you book makes a bigger difference outside.

From a guitar. A guitar sounds warm and clear within about thirty feet, and beyond that it fades quickly. A guitar doesn’t have the low notes to carry very far outdoors, so we’ll usually add a small sound system to keep the music at the same easy level across a wider area, even where we wouldn’t need one indoors.

From a jazz trio. A trio sounds fuller and carries further, because the low notes of an upright bass travel well outdoors. Our drummer plays with brushes for a soft, steady beat, so the trio stays relaxed and easy to talk over.

Timing

How long is a wedding cocktail hour?

A cocktail hour usually runs about 60 minutes. If your ceremony and reception are in the same space, plan on 90 minutes, and the duo can keep playing while your guests wait.

Where the time goes. In a shared space, the venue may need to turn your ceremony rows into dinner tables, and that takes time.

Music through dinner. The duo can keep playing through dinner on the same contract, and if you think cocktail hour might run long, just let us know when you book.

Our duo plays dinner and cocktail parties and client dinners, too.

Should you book live musicians or a DJ for cocktail hour?

A duo can keep an eye on your guests as they arrive and take a request. A DJ is usually a better fit after dinner, and can keep people dancing for hours.

Cocktail hour questions

Looking for the finer details? Take a look at stage sizes and power, how cocktail hour leads into dinner and other common questions.

Do you need music during cocktail hour?
We think so, because a quiet cocktail hour can feel like a gap in the day. We book cocktail hour musicians across Denver and the Colorado mountains.
How many songs for cocktail hour?
You’d need roughly 15 to 20 songs for a 60-minute cocktail hour and closer to 30 for 90 minutes, since a jazz standard usually runs 3 to 4 minutes. In practice, live musicians don’t count songs. They’ll play straight through and make each song longer or shorter to suit your guests.
Will anyone actually notice the music?
Not in the moment, usually. That’s okay, because live music can make your wedding feel thoughtfully planned even when nobody’s listening closely.
Can people talk over it?
Yes, and the duo can bring the volume up or down on the spot as your crowd grows or thins out.
Instrumental or vocals for cocktail hour?
We usually suggest instrumental music for most of the hour. A singer can draw people’s attention to the words, which can make it harder to talk. If you’d like a singer anyway, no worries. We’ll just keep the volume down to match.
Is a duo enough for 60 to 70 guests?
Yes. Two musicians are usually plenty for a crowd of 60 to 70. If your guests will be spread out across a big outdoor space, we’d suggest the trio, so people at the far end can hear the music just as well.
What does an outdoor cocktail hour need?
The musicians will need a level, dry spot with some cover and power nearby, and we’ll plan ahead with you for moving indoors if the weather turns. Colorado evenings can get cold fast after sunset, and that’s hard on both instruments and electronics.
How far ahead should we book?
Whenever you're ready. If the date is coming up sooner than you'd like, no worries, just reach out and we can often make something great work. You'll have a single point of contact for the day, so if your venue needs anything from us, just let us know.
