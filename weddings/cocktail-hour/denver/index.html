Denver weddings

Live music for cocktail hour at a Denver wedding.

The ceremony ends, the couple leaves for photographs, and the guests find a first drink in a hotel lobby, on a gallery floor or on a garden terrace, which is where this city holds its cocktail hours. A jazz duo plays that hour at a volume guests can talk across.

Denver is our home market, so a Denver metro date carries no travel line, and the same duo can keep playing through dinner on the same contract. Where the hour happens

Hotel mezzanines, gallery floors, garden terraces.

The cocktail hour rarely gets the main floor, because the main floor is being set for dinner. It gets the lobby, the mezzanine, the gallery, the terrace, and a duo is built for exactly those: it fits a corner and plays at a level that suits marble as well as open air.

The grand hotels
Downtown hotels usually hold the cocktail hour one floor from dinner. At the Brown Palace it’s under the atrium lobby, and at the Four Seasons and The Oxford it’s in spaces where the banquet team is already busy, so a duo that’s set up before guests arrive fits right in.
The galleries
Space Gallery on Santa Fe Drive is a good example, with concrete floors and white walls. Hard surfaces carry sound further than carpet and drapes do, so the duo starts quieter than it would in a ballroom.
The gardens
Denver Botanic Gardens puts the hour outdoors. The evening gets cold fast after sunset, so we’ll agree a plan for moving indoors ahead of time. A guitar sounds best close to the terrace, and a trio’s upright bass carries to the far edge of the crowd. Learn more on our cocktail hour page.
The mansions and clubs
Grant-Humphreys Mansion and the University Club of Denver spread the crowd across parlors and landings rather than one open floor. The duo sets up within sight of the bar, where the crowd pools, and the far parlors stay usable for conversation.
The industrial floors
Mile High Station holds the ceremony, cocktails and dinner under one roof. The cocktail hour runs at one end of the building while the staff turn the space at the other, and a duo keeps that changeover from feeling like a wait.

Nearly every Denver cocktail hour lands in one of these five kinds of venue, which is why the venue name is the first thing we ask for. Tell us the venue and the date, and we'll come back with a recommendation and a price for that hour.

What does setup look like at a downtown Denver venue?
It's small and early. A duo is one van and one trip: setup runs about 60 minutes and strike 45, through the service entrance, with dock windows and freight elevators settled with the banquet office before the day, so no cases cross the lobby. Where ceremony and reception share a building, the duo is set up and sound-checked before guests are seated. Signet Artists books Denver wedding cocktail hours.
What does the duo need for space, power and parking?
A corner of about 8 by 6 feet and one dedicated 20-amp circuit, and sharing a circuit with catering warmers is the most common cause of trouble. Parking is settled before the day: a hotel dock has a window, a gallery has a curb, and either way the van is gone before guests arrive.
Is there a travel charge for a Denver wedding?
No. Denver is our home market, so a Denver metro date carries no travel charge. Travel is priced with the date only outside the metro area: Boulder and Colorado Springs are a short drive, and mountain venues are a longer one.
Can the duo play an outdoor cocktail hour at a Denver garden venue?
Yes, on a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it, and the move-indoors time written before the day, because Denver evenings get cold fast after sunset. Garden terraces with a pavilion make this the easiest outdoor booking on the calendar.
Is a duo enough for a cocktail hour at a Denver hotel?
Yes, for the guest counts these buildings hold at cocktail hour. Two musicians carry a lobby lounge or a mezzanine at a level guests who have just met can talk across. The trio is the size to book when the crowd spreads across a full ballroom.
