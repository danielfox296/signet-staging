The cost guide

What live music costs in Colorado.

For weddings, Colorado couples budget $2,338 to $2,858 for a live band for their wedding. The Denver market runs about $2,500 to $8,000, with a trio at the cheaper end of the spectrum, and a full band, complete with horns at the expensive end.

Those figures are based on published market pricing research in 2026. This page addresses what the money covers, why the spread is so large, and what can be modified to save money (or spend more).

What the Price covers

With most acts in the Colorado market, the price has two parts. A call-out fee covers everything that stays the same however long your event runs: the drive, load-in, setup, sound check, strike and production. In addition to that, a rate for performance time covers the music. That split is why a 1-hour cocktail set costs more than 1/4th of a 4 hour reception.

Overtime is the next thing to look for on a quote. In the Denver market, acts charge $200 to $400 for each hour beyond the contracted set, so it’s good to know that before your event.

Beyond that, you may have travel beyond the Denver metro area, lodging for the talent if it's outside our metro service area, and a bit more for holiday weekends.

Market context

Why do live music prices vary so much in Colorado?

Live music in Colorado is priced in tiers, and each tier buys a different thing. Reading a number without knowing which tier it came from is how two quotes end up looking like the same purchase.

The marketplace tier
One online booking marketplace shows that the average cost for a Denver party band is $1,150. But the listings show prices that range from $230 to $3,550. You're seeing a price that buys the act, but what else is included (sound, insurance, travel, production) varies listing by listing. Two quotes for the same raw material, can cover two very different evenings.
The middle tier
The Denver market treats the 4-6 piece band as its median: priced at $4,000 to $6,000, and for weddings, a Colorado couple's budget $2,338 to $2,858 for a band is reflected in market prices Colorado-wide.

In the resort corridor, a 5-6 piece band runs $2,500 to $6,500 in Mountain West markets. Nationally, regarding wedding bookings, most couples land in the $3,000 to $8,000 range, with the average sitting around $4,500. Which is very similar to the range private and corporate events are booked at as well.
The production tier
Nationally, a 7-9 piece show band runs $7,500 to $17,000, and full production adds costs like show direction, lighting, staffing and a program designed around the act. For a gala carrying a production budget, it's the right purchase.

What a Signet night comes to depends on the night. A solo musician through a dinner for twelve and a quartet filling a dance floor all night all have different jobs.

jazz trio & Acoustic duo costs

These are the two sizes most Colorado event planners actually book for different parts of the evening, and they're the two most market published rate guides have the least information about. The prices below are published market rates for each.

The jazz trio
Nationally, a trio can run $2,000 to $3,500 for a 4-hour event. For a dinner set rather than a full evening, a jazz trio or acoustic duo runs $500 to $3,000 nationally. The difference in price comes down to how long the sets are and how much production you need. There’s no published price for a trio in Denver on its own. The Denver band range of $2,500 to $8,000 starts at a trio and goes up to a full band with horns, so a trio will be near the bottom of that range.
The acoustic duo
Nationally, an acoustic duo can run $1,500 to $2,500 for a 4-hour event. That’s one of the lowest full-event prices for live musicians in this guide.

A duo can play a dinner or a cocktail hour without needing a stage.
What changes the prices?
Prices change with the number of musicians, how long they play and what the date is.

What is a jazz trio?

It’s three musicians, and what people usually want to know is which instruments. The traditional combos are piano, bass and drums, or organ, guitar and drums. A great setup for a private event is guitar, upright bass and drums played with brushes instead of sticks, because brushes keep the drums quiet enough for guests to talk. A quartet is the same three with a horn or a voice added.

A trio can play at a volume where guests can talk without leaning in (about 60 to 65 dBA at the nearest table), and it can cover a larger space and more guests than a duo at that same volume. In many cases, a trio takes about 12 by 8 feet and one dedicated 20-amp circuit, so it fits a private dining venue as easily as a ballroom. It plays standards, which are the classic songs of jazz.

What each kind of event costs.

Most of the inquiries we get are for one of these four events, and the market prices each one differently.

The corporate holiday party
A cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000 nationally, and a holiday weekend or a December Saturday in Denver can cost 20 to 30 percent more than a standard date. Find out more on our holiday party page.
The client dinner
At a client dinner, the conversation comes first, so the music stays at a volume where your guests can talk easily. Nationally, a cover or acoustic band for a private party runs $3,000 to $8,000, and a smaller act can cost much less. An acoustic duo for a 4-hour event runs $1,500 to $2,500, and a trio runs $2,000 to $3,500. Find out more on our private parties page.

This is the hour between the ceremony and the reception, and it’s the smallest booking on this list. A classical string duo or trio for a ceremony or cocktail hour runs $800 to $2,000 in Denver. Find out more on our cocktail hour page.

Live music cost questions

Wondering about substitutions, power, curfews or outdoor cover? Take a look at our questions for planners.

Why does live music cost more in the mountains?
It mostly comes down to travel and lodging. A band that finishes late in Aspen isn’t driving back over the passes the same night, so the price has to cover the lodging as well as the drive.
Is a band or a DJ cheaper?
Usually a DJ, and the gap narrows the longer your event runs. A Denver DJ with production lighting can run $2,500 to $6,000 for an event, compared with $2,500 to $8,000 for a Denver band. A corporate event can have both, with a duo through dinner and a DJ to keep the dance floor active, and we can put them on one contract.
Does booking early get a lower price?
No. The price is set by the size of the act, the hours and the date, not by when you book.
How much does it cost to hire a band for two hours, or for four?
Two hours usually costs more than half of four, since the drive, the load-in and the sound check are the same for a shorter event.
