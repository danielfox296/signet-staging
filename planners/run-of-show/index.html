For Planners

Run of show templates for a gala, a wedding and a holiday party.

If you've ever put together a run of show, you know the band usually gets one row that says band plays, and the details get filled in by email the week of the event. We've worked those details out here for the three kinds of events we see most. You're welcome to use them, change the times and delete whatever doesn't apply.

How to read these templates

The cue column is the most important one to get right. Someone needs to give the band the go-ahead for each moment of your event. What usually goes wrong is that two people each assume the other one will signal the band, or nobody knows who decides when the speeches are running long.

We'll go over that column at sound check with whoever is holding the master document. Our bandleader takes their cues from that one person.

Two of those rows are worth a second look. A clear 7:00pm call to dinner is a great way to get 400 people from the bar to their seats, and the band can play them in. At the 9:15pm changeover, the quartet can't move from its dinner setup to its dance setup silently, so the DJ plays through that gap.

The row people move most often is 6:45pm. We like to put the first dance right after the entrances, while everyone is already in their seats. If you hold it until after dinner, some guests may have stepped outside or be busy with coffee.

The row to watch at this party is 8:00pm. In our experience, remarks at a holiday party can start late and run long, and by then your guests have usually been at the bar for a while. We'd suggest putting a hard end time in the document and giving it to your speakers ahead of time. We can bring the band back up on time either way.
