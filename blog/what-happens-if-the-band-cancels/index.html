What happens if the band cancels your wedding or event?

A whole band rarely disappears. One musician out and a substitute in is the usual case. Here's how that works, what winter does to a mountain booking, and what to ask before you sign.

Daniel Fox · Founder · November 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • A substitute musician is normal. Ask who picks them and when you'd hear about it.
  • For a mountain event between September and May, ask where the band will be the night before.
  • Read what the contract says happens if the band cancels. It's a different clause from what happens if you do.

Every planner who books live music has the same question somewhere in the back of their mind. What if the band doesn’t show up?

It’s a good question, and most of the answer comes down to a few things you can ask about before you sign.

Why a band cancels #

A band is a handful of people with their own calendars, their own cars and their own colds. Most of what goes wrong is ordinary, like someone getting sick, a family emergency or a van that won’t start. In Colorado, winter roads can get in the way, too.

How a substitute musician works #

Working musicians keep a short list of players who already know their songs. When the bass player is out, the band calls the next bass player on that list. Most guests never notice.

The exception is the person out front. Guests rarely notice a substitute drummer, but a substitute singer can make it feel like a different act. So it’s worth asking who the substitute would be and when you’d hear about it:

  • Who picks the substitute, and do they already know the songs?
  • Will you tell me before the event, and will you tell me why?
  • If the singer or the bandleader is the one who can’t make it, is it still the act I booked?

What winter does to a mountain booking #

From September 1 through May 31, Colorado’s Traction Law is in effect on I-70 between Dotsero and Morrison. That’s the whole drive from Denver to Vail and Beaver Creek. Every car needs tires with at least 3/16 inch of tread rated for winter, all-weather or mud and snow, or chains or an approved traction device. In a bad storm, CDOT moves to the chain law, which it calls “the final safety measure before the highway is closed.”

A band hauling a trailer of gear up from Denver on the afternoon of a Vail wedding is counting on that road. So ask:

  • Is the band driving up the day of, or the day before?
  • If it’s the day of, what time do they leave, and what happens if I-70 closes?
  • Is there a band that lives up there? (For the Vail Valley, we have Quadrasonic.)

What a band's cancellation policy should say #

A music contract has two cancellation clauses. One covers what happens if you cancel, and the other covers what happens if the band cancels. The second one is often much shorter, so read it closely and then ask:

  • If you cancel on me, do I get everything back?
  • Will you help me find a replacement, or am I on my own?
  • What counts as weather or “circumstances beyond our control,” and who decides the event is off?
