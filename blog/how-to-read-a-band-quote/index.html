How to read a band quote

Before you compare two totals, it helps to know what each one covers.

Signet Artists · August 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • You can only compare two quotes once they assume the same number of sets, the same sound setup and the same travel costs.
  • In our experience, sound is what most often separates two totals that otherwise look alike.
  • Travel depends on how far the band is going, from a short trip along the Front Range to a longer one up the I-70 corridor, and the far resorts can add a night’s lodging.
  • Four things cost nothing to ask for and can hold you up for a week if they’re missing: a certificate of insurance, a W-9, one itemized invoice and a personal point of contact.

You’ve got two band quotes side by side, the totals are a few hundred dollars apart, and you’re not sure what you’d be getting for the difference. Each of those totals sits on top of six or seven decisions somebody already made for you, so they can cover very different events.

Here’s what each of those decisions is, and how to spot it on a quote. This works on any band’s quote, including ours.

How band pricing works #

Most band pricing has two parts. One part covers everything the band has to do no matter how long they play, like travel, load-in, setup, sound check, strike, load-out and production. The other part covers performance time by the hour.

Those are usually called the call-out and the hourly rate. Want to see what the market charges for each configuration? Check out what live music costs for a corporate event. If a quote shows one flat price, ask which part is which, and the rest of the conversation gets easier.

How band size affects the price #

Ensemble size usually moves a band’s price more than anything else, and it’s also the line most often described in words rather than as a number of musicians. “Full band” doesn’t tell you how many players you’re getting. Four musicians and six musicians mean different budgets, and they take up different amounts of space.

Ask the band to put the number of musicians in writing, then check that number against your space. A venue that seats 120 and a venue that seats 400 call for different ensemble sizes. Learn more about the space each size takes up in what a band needs from the venue.

How sound changes a band quote #

Some bands include a sound system that fits your venue, some assume the venue has one, and some assume you’ll hire an engineer separately. All three are normal ways to work, and they can lead to very different totals.

We can bring the right sound system for your venue and keep music playing during breaks, too. If you’d like stage lighting or a dedicated engineer, just let us know.

When you compare two quotes, sound is the first thing to even out. Once both quotes assume the same sound setup, the gap between them may close or even flip.

How many sets you get, and what happens if the music runs long #

Check how many sets you’re getting, how long each one is, and what an extra half hour would cost if you want to keep going.

Events often run long and most bands set those terms in advance, so it’s worth asking before you book.

Travel and lodging costs for a band #

Distance is a real cost, and a good quote shows it before you have to ask. We look at travel by area, and dates along the Front Range, like Boulder, Colorado Springs and Fort Collins, are the shortest trips. The I-70 corridor, including Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge and Winter Park, is a longer drive. For the far resorts, like Aspen, Telluride, Steamboat and Crested Butte, you can expect travel plus a night’s lodging, because we don’t want a band that packs up late driving that road home.

When you read a quote for a mountain date, look for where the travel is covered, and just ask if you can’t find it.

What paperwork to ask a band for #

Four items don’t add anything to the price, but they can cost you a week when they’re missing: a certificate of insurance, a W-9, one itemized invoice and a personal point of contact for your event.

We can take care of all four, so just let us know what your venue needs. With any band, it’s worth confirming all four before you pay a deposit, because your venue may ask for the insurance certificate the week of your event.

How to compare two band quotes #

First, put both quotes on an even footing, with the same number of sets, the same sound setup and the same travel costs, and add anything that’s missing from either one. Only then look at the two prices.

Most of the time the exercise doesn’t change which band you want. It can show you what you’re actually choosing between, though, and that helps a lot if you need to explain the cost to someone else.
