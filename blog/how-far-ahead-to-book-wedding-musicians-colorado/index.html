How far ahead to book wedding musicians in Colorado

Whenever you're ready is the short answer. What helps more is the order the decisions usually come in, what a booking involves, and the eight things to have ready before you write to anybody.

Signet Artists · September 6, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • You can write to us whenever you're ready. We hear from couples more than a year ahead and a few weeks before the day, and we're glad to help either way.
  • The venue decision comes first and settles most of the music decisions for you: the sound cap, the curfew, the power, and whether the ceremony is outdoors.
  • A song we learn specially for you is the one thing that takes extra time, so let us know about it when you book.
  • Eight things can get your inquiry answered in one email instead of four, starting with the date and the venue.

Somebody has just said yes, there’s a venue with a date on it, and somewhere on a list between the photographer and the flowers is the word music. How far ahead you book the music is up to you. Here’s how we’d work through it.

Wedding music decisions, in the order they usually come #

The date and the venue. Just about everything else follows from these two. Ask the venue for three things while you’re still deciding: the decibel cap if it has one, the time music has to stop, and where the power is for anything happening outdoors.

How the day splits. Most weddings have a ceremony, a cocktail hour, dinner and dancing. Many couples don’t need the same music for all four, and most of the hours are conversation rather than dancing. Once you’ve decided how to split the day, it’s much easier to set a budget for the music.

The ceremony. You’ll want to choose the processional and the recessional, since they’re the part of the day where the music has to come in right on cue. A solo guitarist or a duo can play a ceremony and then move to the cocktail hour, so you only have to book one act.

The cocktail hour. Live musicians can make a big difference here, and it’s the hour couples most often skip. Find out more on our cocktail hour page, including whether you need live music for it and what it should sound like.

The reception. You can choose a band, a DJ or both, one after the other, and many couples with a full evening go with both. Want some help deciding? Take a look at band or DJ for a Denver wedding.

What booking a musician involves #

In our experience it involves less than most couples expect, and the parts that take time are the parts nobody warns you about.

The inquiry is short. A date, a venue, roughly how many guests, and which parts of the day you want covered is enough for us to reply with availability and a price.

Until a contract is signed, nothing is settled with any vendor.

Then comes the paperwork, which is the step that surprises people. Many Colorado venues won’t confirm a vendor until they have a certificate of insurance naming the venue as additional insured. Ask your venue whether it requires one. We can provide one and name your venue on it, so just let us know that’s something you need.

The details can wait until much later. We’ll settle song choices, the processional, a do-not-play list and the flow of the day with you in the weeks before the wedding.

Eight things to have ready when you contact wedding musicians #

If your inquiry includes these, we can usually answer it in one email.

  1. The date, and whether it’s fixed or still moving.
  2. The venue and its address, or the two you’re choosing between.
  3. Roughly how many guests.
  4. Which parts of the day you want music for.
  5. Whether any of it is outdoors, and whether there’s cover and power.
  6. The venue’s decibel cap and the time music has to stop, if it has given you either.
  7. Whether the venue requires a certificate of insurance.
  8. Anything you already know you want or definitely don’t want.

Numbers five and six are the ones that change the answer most, and they’re the two couples almost never think to include. If your venue asks for stage space, power and load-in details in writing, you’ll find them by size on our technical page.

If the wedding is coming up soon #

If the wedding is coming up sooner than you’d like, no worries. Just reach out and we can often make something great work. Send the date and the venue and we’ll come back with what we can do for that day.

The one thing that takes a little longer is a song learned specially for you, since we usually need a few weeks before the wedding to learn new material. With less notice than that, we’d stick to the songs an act already plays, and that can work out even better, because a band plays a song it knows better than one it learned last week. Curious what our musicians already play? Check out our repertoire page.

If the wedding is in the mountains #

A mountain wedding takes a little more planning, mostly because of travel rather than the music. The band may run into weather on the drive up, there can be a loading dock or a shuttle at the venue, and sometimes they’ll need a night’s lodging. The altitude can also change how long a sound check takes. None of that is hard. It just helps to tell us the venue when you write, so the travel is in the plan from the start.

Find out more about travel to mountain venues on our mountain page. If you’re planning the whole wedding weekend, we’re glad to help with the welcome party and the rehearsal dinner, too.

Send the date and the venue, whatever the timeline looks like.

We’ll let you know if that date is open and what the parts of the day you want covered will cost, and we may have a few questions, too.
