Company picnic entertainment ideas in Denver

Here are eight ideas for your summer company picnic, from a band on the lawn to a party on the patio, with the shelter and power each one needs.

Signet Artists · September 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways
  • A company picnic often has to work for families in the afternoon and for the crowd that stays into the evening, and those two groups may want very different entertainment.
  • In Colorado, the afternoon weather is the first thing to plan around. Any stage should be covered, level and dry, with power run to it, and it's smart to decide ahead of time when you'd move indoors.
  • The lists you find for a company picnic are games lists. Live music is missing from those lists, and everyone on the lawn can enjoy it at once.
  • If your picnic turns into a party at dusk, we can book it all together, with an acoustic act through the afternoon and a band or a DJ when the sun goes down.

If you planned the holiday party, there’s a good chance you’re planning the company picnic, too. It has to work for the kids and for the sales team, in the sun and possibly in the rain, on a lawn where the nearest outlet may be a long way off. If you search for company picnic ideas, you’ll mostly find lists of games. Those lists usually leave out live music, which everyone on the lawn can enjoy at once without waiting in line.

The Americana band on the lawn #

An Americana band is a great choice for an afternoon in the sun. It has acoustic guitar, fiddle, upright bass and drums, and it plays Colorado favorites like the Grateful Dead and Tom Petty alongside Nathaniel Rateliff and the Lumineers. It’s soft enough for people to chat at the picnic tables, and the same band can get people dancing in the evening. Want to know more? Take a look at our Americana band.

The acoustic duo by the food line #

An acoustic duo is voice and guitar, playing covers people know at a volume they can easily talk over. We like to set up near the food, since that’s where people tend to gather at a picnic. An acoustic duo is small enough to fit the corner of a pavilion.

The DJ afternoon #

A DJ is one of the simplest options to approve and to run, with one line on the budget and one load-in. They can play through the whole picnic, and you’ll have a microphone for the raffle. An open-format DJ plays all kinds of music, so they can start with songs for the families and get people dancing later on.

The yacht rock evening #

If your picnic is really more of a summer party on a patio or a rooftop, book the yacht rock band. It plays songs by the Doobie Brothers, Steely Dan, Hall and Oates and Toto, with great vocal harmonies and the saxophone out front, and it’s perfect if your crowd likes to sing along. A duo can also play through the afternoon before the band starts.

The solo guitarist under the pavilion #

For a smaller company or a department picnic, a solo guitarist can set up under the pavilion roof and play through the afternoon. They need very little from your site, too.

Company picnic games and activities for adults and large groups #

You might have lawn games for adults, an inflatable for the kids, a tug of war or a cornhole bracket. These are all great fun for the people playing them. We’d suggest doing both, since live music can tie the whole afternoon together, including for the people who aren’t playing.

Company picnic entertainment for families and the evening crowd #

Many company picnics end up feeling like two events with the same guest list. The afternoon is for families, so you’ll want relaxed music that works for all ages. Later on, the people who stay may want a place to dance. We can book all of that for you together, with an acoustic act or the Americana trio through the afternoon, and the same band with drums, the yacht rock band or the DJ when the sun goes down. It’s all one contract. Learn more about how we work with companies on our corporate page.

What an outdoor stage needs in Colorado #

For an outdoor stage, we’ll need a covered, level, dry spot with power run to it, and we can work out a plan with you ahead of time for moving indoors. Colorado afternoons can turn fast, and instruments and electronics don’t do well in the rain, so a pavilion or a tent with a deck can keep the band playing. Want the details on stage sizes and power?
