The pianist in the store

In 1985, Nordstrom started hiring pianists to play in its stores. Here’s what those pianists did for shoppers, and how Nordstrom phased them out.

Signet Artists · November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • Nordstrom started hiring pianists in 1985, and at one point about three-quarters of its stores had one.
  • Pianists like Joel Baker took requests and got to know the regulars, and Baker played one couple’s favorite song every time they came in.
  • Nordstrom phased the pianists out over many years, and the last one retired shortly before the pandemic.

You know the sound of a piano somewhere off to the side of a hotel lobby while you check in. For years, Nordstrom shoppers heard the same thing while they shopped.

According to former Nordstrom pianist Joel Baker, the company started hiring pianists in 1985, for its newly remodeled and expanded store at Bellevue Square, outside Seattle. In the years that followed, as many as three-quarters of Nordstrom’s stores had a pianist, playing jazz and Broadway standards. Baker played at the Southcenter Nordstrom, south of Seattle, from 1988 to 2003.

What did a Nordstrom pianist do? #

A pianist in a store could take requests and get to know the regulars. One older couple asked Baker to play their favorite song, Irving Berlin’s “Always,” and every time they came in, he played it for them.

Then the husband passed away. One day his widow came back to the piano alone. Baker wasn’t sure whether he should play the song, because he didn’t want to make her sad. She asked him for it, and he played it, and he kept playing it for her whenever he saw her after that.

Why did Nordstrom stop having pianists? #

Nordstrom let the pianists go slowly, one store at a time. In 2007, the Seattle Times reported that the Bellevue Square store had recently let its pianist go, and that the store at Alderwood mall in Lynnwood would soon do the same. Some shoppers, the paper reported, preferred popular tunes by the likes of Bob Dylan, Alicia Keys and Frank Sinatra. About half of the chain’s 101 stores still had a pianist at that point.

By 2013, only 30 of Nordstrom’s more than 110 stores had a pianist, hired part-time for two days a week, and a company spokesperson called recorded music “more modern.” Baker remembers the company as always trying to keep the stores relevant and contemporary.

The last Nordstrom pianist, Joe Rojo, retired shortly before the pandemic, and the company didn’t replace him. Baker still plays piano professionally, now in Palm Springs. He says he hopes the music made some kind of a difference in people’s lives in some small way.

What can a live pianist do that a playlist can’t? #

A pianist can read the room. They can see who’s there and play to it, a little softer when the floor is quiet and a little brighter when it’s busy, and they can take a request or remember a regular’s favorite song. A playlist plays the same way whether the store is empty or full.
