Band or DJ for a Denver wedding or company party

Here's what a band and a DJ each bring to an event, what each one costs across Colorado, and why many hosts planning a full evening end up booking both.

Signet Artists · September 6, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways
  • A band gives your guests something to watch as well as dance to, while a DJ keeps people dancing without a break.
  • A DJ playing a whole night with production lighting can run $2,500 to $6,000 across Colorado. Denver bands can run $2,500 to $8,000, with a trio at the low end and a full band with horns at the high end.
  • A DJ plays recordings, so if your venue or program asks for live musicians, you'll need a band or a live instrument playing over the DJ set.
  • For a full evening, many hosts book both, with live musicians through arrival and dinner and a DJ for the late hours.

At some point in planning a wedding or a company party, somebody asks whether you’re getting a band or a DJ. A band puts real musicians in front of your guests, and watching them play is part of the fun. A DJ can play the song everybody’s been waiting for at just the right moment, mix it right into the next one and keep people dancing.

Both make for a great party, and they feel different, so we’d start with how you want your event to feel. We book bands and we book DJs, so we’re happy with either answer.

Is a DJ considered live music? #

No. A DJ plays and mixes recordings, and live music means musicians playing or singing in the venue. People ask us this a lot, and it matters because some venues, ceremonies and corporate programs ask for live musicians, and a DJ won’t meet that request.

The performance is real, though. A DJ watches the dance floor and decides what to play next, which takes real skill, even with recorded music. We also often book a bassist or a saxophonist to play live over the DJ set, which gives you some of both. That’s live music, and it can keep the dance floor active after a band would already have gone home.

Band vs DJ cost in Denver #

In the Colorado market, a DJ with production lighting for a whole night can cost $2,500 to $6,000. Denver bands can cost $2,500 to $8,000, with a trio at the low end and a full band with horns at the high end. Bands of four to six musicians, the middle of the market, can cost $4,000 to $6,000. For corporate events, a cover or jazz band can cost $2,500 to $8,000, and a jazz trio or acoustic duo for a dinner can cost $500 to $3,000.

Those prices overlap. A small live act and a well-equipped DJ can cost about the same, and what moves the price most for either one is how many people are working and for how long.

Two other costs can come up with either one. In the Colorado market, a holiday weekend can add 20 to 30 percent, and across Denver, an hour beyond the contracted set can cost $200 to $400.

Is a wedding DJ worth it? #

For most receptions, yes, and the main reason is how long a DJ can play. A DJ can cover the whole night without a break, play a song just the way your guests know it, and handle the announcements, too. If your priority is a dance floor that stays active from dinner to the end, a DJ is usually your best bet.

A DJ may not be the right choice for every occasion, though. Live musicians can make an occasion feel special, because there are people right in front of your guests doing something difficult in real time. You’ll notice that most during the parts of the day when people aren’t dancing anyway, like the ceremony, the cocktail hour and the seated dinner.

Booking a band and a DJ in sequence #

With this plan, live musicians play the first part of the evening and a DJ takes over for the second half. It’s the most common configuration we book, and it’s often the most affordable way to get both. The band and the DJ can share one sound system on one contract, so the music doesn’t have to stop between them.

At a wedding, that might mean an acoustic duo or a jazz trio for the ceremony and cocktail hour, the band for the first dances and the early dancing, and then the DJ for the rest of the night. A corporate event can work the same way, with dinner music your guests can talk over and dancing after, and that’s a standard plan for a Denver holiday party. Learn more on our DJ page. Would you rather have one act grow with the evening? Take a look at Tejas Singh, who can play as a duo through arrival and dinner and grow to a quartet when the dancing starts.

How to choose between a band and a DJ #

We’d look at five things, in the order they usually matter.

The dance floor. Think about how many people are likely to dance, and for how long. If your crowd is likely to dance late, you’ll probably want a DJ somewhere in the plan, because a band plays sets with breaks in them.

The venue’s rules. A decibel limit or an early curfew can change your answer more than taste does. Ask your venue for both in writing before you book either one, and we’ll plan the music around them. Need technical details for your venue? Take a look at our technical page, which lists space, power and sound needs by ensemble size.

The parts of the event when nobody’s dancing. The ceremony, cocktails, dinner and speeches can take up most of the day. Live musicians do more for those hours than they do for a dance floor.

Who’s in the crowd. A DJ is a great choice for a wide range of ages. If your guests would love to watch a performance, we’d lean toward a band.

The budget. If your budget is fixed and the event runs long, a smaller live act plus a DJ can cover more of the evening than a big band on its own.

What we recommend #

If the event has a program in it, such as a client dinner, an awards night or a seminar with a reception after, book live musicians and skip the DJ. The music can sit under the conversation and stop for the speeches, and you won’t need a dance floor.

If your event is a party from start to finish and it runs late, book the DJ, and add a live instrument over the DJ set if you’d like something for people to watch as well as dance to.

If it’s a wedding or a corporate event that runs the whole evening, book both in sequence, and have the live musicians play during the parts of the event when people are talking.
