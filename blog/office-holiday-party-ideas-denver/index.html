Office holiday party entertainment ideas in Denver

Here are the formats a Denver office party can take, plus what changes for a large group or a January party.

Signet Artists · August 27, 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways
  • Each idea on this list comes with the size of act it needs, so you can bring your team the idea and the budget conversation at the same time.
  • A cover or jazz band for a corporate event can run $2,500 to $8,000 in the Colorado market, and that's a good range to bring into a budget conversation.
  • A December Saturday in Denver usually carries a 20 to 30 percent markup, and it's best to ask us about New Year's Eve.
  • The venue is a separate decision from the entertainment.

The remarks are done, the lights come down a little, and the band that played through dinner is suddenly loud enough to dance to. Getting a party to that moment is mostly a budget problem. Most lists of ideas don't come with prices, so every option you bring back starts a new round of research into what it costs, and that can really slow your planning down.

Each idea below comes with the size of act and the hours it needs, so you can see what you're pricing. As for the budget, a cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000 in the Colorado market, and Denver bands fall in that same range, with a trio at the bottom and a full band with horns at the top. Want to know what goes into those prices? Read what live music costs for a corporate event, or take a look at the market prices on our pricing page.

Our artists have played for Amazon, NBCUniversal, Charles Schwab, Kroger, the Denver Art Museum, Sheraton, Hyatt, the Washington Nationals, Casa Bonita and Monarch Casino, and the formats below are what we'd suggest for events like those.

Christmas party entertainment that leaves space for the conversation #

Ask a group of planners what to bring to a Christmas party and you get magicians, mentalists, tarot readers and a casino night, and then somebody says the whole category is played out. What they’re after is simpler than that list makes it look. They want something guests can gather around that still leaves space for conversation, and the case they make for a magician is that it gives guests a reason to stop and talk to each other.

A trio can do that for your whole event. With guitar, upright bass and drums played with brushes, the music stays at a level where your colleagues can easily talk. Nobody has to take part, and anyone who wants to can wander over and listen for a while. That gives the party a center without anyone having to organize it.

That’s also the answer to the fear of forced fun. A band isn’t an activity your team has to sign up for, and live music can help the party keep going after the catering has been cleared.

A DJ for the office holiday party #

In our experience, a DJ is the most common choice for an office holiday party, and it can be the easiest one to get approved, since one vendor covers the whole event. An open-format DJ plays all kinds of music and can change it up to suit your crowd.

This is the idea to pitch when you need to stretch your budget. Across the Colorado market, a DJ with production lighting for the whole event runs $2,500 to $6,000, which is often less than a full band for the same hours, and a DJ can still get people dancing.

A jazz duo for a holiday dinner #

This is a great choice when the party is really a dinner, with everyone seated, a toast or two, and conversation as the main event. Our jazz duo plays jazz standards on upright bass and guitar, at a volume where nobody has to lean in to talk. Three hours is the usual length.

If your group is too big for a duo, a jazz trio adds a drummer and can reach a bigger space. A jazz trio or acoustic duo for a dinner can run $500 to $3,000 in the Colorado market.

Live music at the office party #

Not every office party leaves the office, and if yours is a catered spread at the end of the workday, a small act is a great fit. You can book the acoustic duo for a single hour, with voice and guitar playing covers people know, at a volume your team can easily talk over. A solo guitarist is a smaller option still, and can fit on an office floor without anyone moving the desks.

A live band for dancing #

A quartet can play a four-hour event, keeping the volume down through dinner so people can talk, then turning it up when the program ends. This is the one to book when dancing is the whole idea, and in our experience, four musicians is the right size for a ballroom dance floor. Adding horns and a second vocalist moves you into show-band prices, at $7,500 to $17,000.

One of our favorite ways to do this is with Tejas Singh, who can start as a duo through arrival and dinner and grow to a quartet when the dancing starts, with the same voice and the same songs all the way through. It sounds like one band getting bigger.

A live band, then a DJ #

This is a great option for a crowd that will want to keep going after the band is done, with a quartet for three hours and then a DJ playing the rest of the party through the same sound system. Since the sound system is already set up, adding that last hour can cost a lot less than booking a separate act. You get two different kinds of music at one party, and we can take care of both.

Live band karaoke #

The band plays 80s and 90s hits with a singer out front, and anyone in the crowd who wants a song can take the microphone, with the words on a screen and a host on the floor. The band can keep the music going on its own, and it’s ready the moment somebody gets brave. You can book it for your whole event or as the loud part of a longer one. Curious how it works? A flamenco show for the holiday party #

Some planners want one big moment, something their team hasn’t seen at an office party before. Dirty Flamenco is a great choice for that. They’re a flamenco trio who play the traditional forms of flamenco, and we can add a dancer if you’d like a floor show. This is a fun way to make the entertainment the centerpiece of your party.

Holiday party entertainment ideas for large groups #

Most of these ideas work for bigger groups, but not all in the same way, and your headcount is what decides it. A small dinner and a packed ballroom are very different when it comes to sound, and the ballroom will need a bigger band.

Under about eighty guests, a duo can play the whole event on its own. Two musicians can set up in a corner without any fuss, and the music can still reach every table.

Between eighty and about a hundred and fifty, the trio is the size to book. Drums played with brushes let a trio reach about twice the space a duo can, and your guests can still talk. In our experience, this is the size most large Denver office parties need, and it’s smaller than the band most planners ask about first.

Above a hundred and fifty, we’d suggest a quartet, especially if you want people dancing.

Two things can go wrong with a large group, whichever size of band you choose. The first is that a large group can break into lots of separate conversations soon after arriving, so entertainment that only reaches the people lined up for it will miss most of your guest list. The second is the venue’s own audio. A venue’s own sound system is often set up for speeches and may not carry a band across a full ballroom, so we’ll size ours to fit the venue.

How the date changes holiday party entertainment prices #

Your date can change the price a lot. Most holiday party dates fall Thursday through Saturday across November and December, and a peak December date costs more than a standard one across Colorado, with a Denver holiday weekend usually carrying a 20 to 30 percent markup. New Year’s Eve is a peak date too, so just ask us about that one.

The Christmas party that happens in January #

Plenty of Denver companies now hold their Christmas party after the new year, and the planners who moved it tend to give the same reason. December is packed with other parties, and in January nobody on your team has to choose between yours and another one that same evening.

Everything on this list works in January, and the bigger ideas can work even better. Without the rest of the holidays competing for your team’s attention, an act people will want to watch can really shine, like the flamenco trio, live band karaoke or a quartet for dancing. The music also suits a January date, since jazz standards and covers aren’t holiday songs.
