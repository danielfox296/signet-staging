Where to hold a corporate holiday party in Denver in 2026

Here are sixteen venues with real private-event pages, from a mezzanine over Union Station to a hangar that holds 3,500, along with the notes we&rsquo;d give a client on each one.

Signet Artists · August 21, 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways
  • You&rsquo;ll find the most choice in the middle, for parties of 75 to 200 guests.
  • Five of these venues already have live music as a regular part of what they do, so a band fits right in.
  • Budgets tend to slip in the fine print, so watch for in-house catering rules, approved-caterer lists, evening start times and one November maintenance closure.

So you’ve been put in charge of the holiday party, and the first question is where to hold it. Here’s the short answer. For a big party, take a look at Mission Ballroom, Wings Over the Rockies or Mile High Station. For most parties of 75 to 200 people, you’ve got plenty of choices, from the Brown Palace and Ironworks to Space Gallery, Ophelia’s, Number Thirty Eight and Nocturne, and the Manor House and Boettcher Mansion are lovely if you’d prefer an estate. Under eighty guests, the Cooper Lounge, Vauxhall and the Clyfford Still Museum are the three we’d send a planner to first.

We’re a booking agency, so we spend a lot of time in spaces like these, paying attention to how the sound works and where a band fits. Every capacity number comes from the venue’s own published event pages as of August 2026, and where a venue doesn’t publish something, we say so and suggest you ask.

Large venues for 200 guests and up #

Mission Ballroom, RiNo. This is a concert hall that holds 3,950 standing at full scale and around 450 for a seated banquet, with reception layouts from 300 to 2,000 on a 9,000 square foot floor. Private events get the venue’s own concert sound and lighting, a 40 by 20 foot LED wall and four full bars. This may be the one venue in Denver where a band can plug right into a sound system built for touring acts.

Wings Over the Rockies, Lowry. This is a WWII-era hangar of 44,000 square feet that seats up to 1,200 and holds 3,500 standing, with the aircraft arranged around your layout and about 300 free parking spaces outside. Built-in sound and lighting come with the rental. A hangar is a long space that echoes, so you’ll want a sound system sized for it, with the dance floor close to the stage. The venue works from a preferred-caterer list, and flames, fog and confetti need pre-approval.

Mile High Station, West Colfax by Empower Field. This former steel and ironworks plant is on the National Register and can host 100 to 1,200 guests, with 550 seated across the main floor and mezzanine. There’s in-house sound, and music performances are on the venue’s own list of uses for the building. Brick and steel can sound great with a loud band, and the mezzanine gives people somewhere to go if they’d rather talk.

Two more venues of this size are worth a look. Asterisk, a 1923 post-office garage near Ballpark, holds 1,000 standing or 600 dining. It includes house sound, a wireless mic and preset stage lighting, and you can bring any caterer, which can really help your budget. The Denver Art Museum hosts up to 1,000 with gallery access included, and there are two rules to plan around. Catering is in-house only, and evening events start no earlier than 6 pm.

Mid-size venues for 75 to 200 guests #

The Brown Palace, downtown. The Brown Palace has nineteen spaces totaling more than 30,000 square feet, and the full ballroom seats 400 theater-style. The hotel has been throwing December parties since 1892. The hotel has its own live jazz in the atrium every Friday and Saturday evening, so a band feels right at home here.

Ironworks, by Ball Arena. This is Mile High Station’s 1903 sister building, which hosts 50 to 350 guests, with original brick and a turret, onsite parking and local beer on tap. Live performances are on the venue’s own list of uses, too.

Space Gallery, Art District on Santa Fe. This art gallery holds 300 standing or 250 seated across the main floor, mezzanine and sculpture garden, and it has the most planner-friendly policies in this guide. You can bring your own caterer and your own beverages, and the rental is one flat published price for an eight-hour day rather than a quote. Their events page says bands and DJs are welcome, and they can bring equipment or plug into the included AV.

Ophelia’s Electric Soapbox, Ballpark. This is a multi-level venue with a dedicated Stage Level and published buyout pricing. The full building holds 130 seated or 425 cocktail-style, and you can rent just the Stage Level for a smaller party. It hosts live music several nights a week, so the sound, sightlines and stage are already taken care of.

Nocturne, RiNo. This jazz supper club offers full buyouts from 25 to 140, with art deco style against warehouse brick, and its own pages promote corporate holiday parties. One thing to know is that Nocturne books its own music from its roster, so if you have a band in mind, it’s best to talk with them first.

Number Thirty Eight, RiNo. This former neon-sign factory has more than 31,000 square feet inside and out, with semi-private groups of 30 to 200 during regular hours and full buyouts above that. With permanent stages indoors and out and free live music most nights, it’s the most casual of the venues built around music, and there are 100 Colorado taps behind the bar.

If you’d like a holiday party at an estate, there are two great options just outside Denver. The Manor House in Littleton’s Ken Caryl Valley seats up to 200 for a formal dinner in a 1914 mansion with downtown views from its six acres, in-house catering, and a sound system in the ballroom. Boettcher Mansion on Lookout Mountain takes up to 150 in a 1917 Arts and Crafts lodge at 7,550 feet, run by Jefferson County. The county’s calendar shows the mansion closed for maintenance November 23 through 26, 2026, so Thanksgiving week is out this year.

Small venues for under 80 guests #

The Cooper Lounge, Union Station. The Cooper Lounge seats forty to eighty on the mezzanine behind the 28-foot windows of the 1914 Great Hall, and the Crawford Hotel’s event team runs service. Guests can take the train home. For music, a solo musician or a duo suits a space this size.

Vauxhall at the Ramble Hotel, RiNo. This 2,300 square foot venue has its own street entrance, marquee and valet, and it seats roughly 70. The hotel’s bar program is run by Death and Co. The venue advertises its own AV, so ask about amplified music when you book.

Clyfford Still Museum, Golden Triangle. You can host a dinner for 50 or a reception for 150 among all nine galleries of one artist’s life work. The venue’s own page lists rental rates for a four-hour evening buyout or a longer Monday option. The museum has its own concert programming, and its event rentals include AV, but it’s worth asking about the amplified-music policy for private events.

Holiday party venues with live music built in #

If live music is the main reason for your party, five of these venues make the decision easy, because live performance is part of what they do every week. Mission Ballroom has a sound system built for touring acts, Nocturne has jazz on its stage every night it’s open, Ophelia’s has its Stage Level and a public show calendar, Number Thirty Eight has indoor and outdoor stages, and the Brown Palace has jazz every weekend. Space Gallery and Mile High Station deserve a mention too, since their own published policies say they welcome bands and have what a band needs.

Everywhere else, it’s safest to assume the house sound system was set up for speeches, so we’ll size our sound to the venue for any ensemble you book. A band that brings its own sound system can make choosing a venue simpler, because you won’t have to rely on what the venue already has.

The fine print that can change your budget

Four kinds of fine print come up across this list, and you won’t usually find them on the first page of a brochure. The first is catering. The Denver Art Museum is in-house only, and Wings and Boettcher work from approved lists. The second is start time, since evening events at the Denver Art Museum begin at 6 pm or later. The third is the calendar, since Boettcher Mansion is closed November 23 to 26, 2026. The fourth is privacy, since Number Thirty Eight considers 200-plus a full buyout, and below that you’ll be sharing the building. All of these are easy to plan around if you know about them ahead of time.

Holiday party dates in Denver #

Fridays and the two Saturdays in mid-December tend to be the most popular venue dates, and venues that put on public shows, which includes several above, set their private dates around their own calendars.

Live music on a December Saturday in Denver usually costs 20 to 30 percent more than on a standard date. If your date is already set, just send it to us with the venue, and we’ll check it.
