Company offsite ideas in Colorado for remote teams

Remote teams often get together for just one week a year. Here's how they plan a company offsite, where they go in Colorado, and seven great ideas for the evenings.

Signet Artists · September 14, 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways
  • The people who organize company offsites are overwhelmingly remote or hybrid themselves: 91 percent of offsite organizers work that way, and 57 percent of fully remote companies call the offsite essential to the business.
  • Most companies gather once or twice a year for a few days, and September is the busiest month. Winter offsites are on the rise, and in Colorado that usually means an evening in a ski town.
  • The days usually go to meetings and team building. The evenings often get left blank, and that's when people who have only met on video can get to know each other.
  • A third of planners now report more demand for locally flavored events, and Colorado has plenty of local flavor to offer.

When your company doesn't have an office, the offsite is about the only time everyone's in the same place. It’s the one week a year, sometimes two, when people who have worked together for years finally shake hands, and you’re the one hoping it all goes well.

A lot of companies do this now. 91 percent of the people organizing company offsites work in a fully remote or hybrid arrangement themselves, 57 percent of fully remote companies say the offsite is essential to the success of the business, and 70 percent of companies hold one or two a year. The average offsite runs about four and a half days and three and a half nights, on weekdays, with team building named as the primary objective by 85 percent of companies. That makes sense when you look at how people work now. 52 percent of remote-capable employees in the United States work hybrid and 26 percent work exclusively remote, so flying everyone in to see each other isn’t unusual anymore.

What a company offsite agenda usually holds #

The industry reports and the conversations planners have online mostly tell the same story. Nearly every group has meeting space where they’re staying. Meals happen there too, and 92 percent of groups eat three or more of them on site. The average stay has two team-building activities in it. September is the single most popular month, with 18 percent of the year’s offsites, and December-to-March bookings are up 21 percent year over year. Budgets are going up too, and 59 percent of companies have increased what they spend on offsites since 2019.

When engineers and managers ask each other about offsites, the advice that keeps coming up is not to overbook the evenings. After a full day of meetings, most people don’t want a second program at dinner. They want a good meal, a drink and a relaxed reason to spend time with people they’ve only met on video, and live music can give them that.

Where remote teams go in Colorado #

Colorado is an easy place to get everyone to. A distributed team can fly into Denver from most of the country without a connection, and you can be up in the mountains within two hours of landing. If you search for a Colorado company retreat, you’ll mostly find venues, and they fall into four groups.

The guest ranches. Devil’s Thumb Ranch outside Tabernash lists barns and pavilions for 40 to 250 people, with fly fishing, zip lines, ropes courses and horseback riding for team building, and s’mores every night. C Lazy U near Granby sells all-inclusive group rates with lodging, meals and ranch activities in one price. This is a great fit if you want the whole company staying on the property, with a barn right there for the evenings.

The mountain-town basecamps. Gravity Haus markets its Breckenridge, Vail, Winter Park, Steamboat Springs and Denver locations for, in its own words, “remote board meetings, company retreats, and team bonding,” with coworking space, restaurants and bars and gear rental in one building. It’s an easy way to put your whole team right in the middle of a mountain town.

The resort towns. Vail, Beaver Creek, Aspen and Breckenridge all have hotels that host groups year-round, and those hotels may write a sound limit and a curfew into your contract. Learn more about the drive, the lodging and the sound rules for each town on our retreats page.

Denver itself. If your company flies in and stays downtown, the convention-center hotels and the city’s own venues can host the working days, and the mountains are just a drive away. A growing number of remote-first companies are already based in Colorado, and Google even suggests “remote companies colorado” as you start typing.

Arrival night: live acoustic music at the first dinner #

The first evening of an offsite can feel a little strange for a remote company. Everyone knows each other’s faces from video calls, and suddenly they’re all standing in one place. An acoustic duo or the Americana trio is a great choice for this first night. They’ll keep the music low enough for everyone to catch up, and it gives people something to gather around. Nobody has to do anything but show up and say hello.

The Colorado evening: live Americana music #

A third of planners now report more demand for locally flavored events, 34 percent in the most recent industry forecast, and if your team has flown in from all over, it’s nice to give them one evening that feels like Colorado. The Americana band plays the Grateful Dead, Tom Petty and Neil Young alongside Colorado songwriters like Nathaniel Rateliff and Gregory Alan Isakov, on acoustic guitar, fiddle, upright bass and drums. In a ranch barn, the same band can play through dinner and then get people dancing.

Live band karaoke, the remote team's icebreaker #

Karaoke is near the top of the offsite ideas lists that big employers publish, and it’s a great fit for a remote team. With a live band behind them, your coworkers can get up and sing together. The band plays plenty of 80s and 90s hits on its own, and anyone who wants a turn can grab the mic, with the words on a screen and a host there to help. Learn more about our live band karaoke.

The last night: a dance party for the whole company #

A lot of offsites end with one evening that’s just a party. The yacht rock band is an excellent choice if your team loves to sing along, and the soul and R&B band is great for getting people dancing in a ballroom. You could also book a DJ with a live musician playing along. If your team wants to keep dancing after the band’s last set, a DJ can take over on the same sound system, and it’s all one contract for the whole stay.

The winter offsite: an apres-ski evening #

December-to-March offsites are on the rise, and in Colorado you barely need a theme, since the snow is right outside the window. A few great options are the Americana band, a solo pianist through dinner, or live band karaoke in a ski-town bar that you’ve bought out for the evening. Headed to Vail? Take a look at our Vail evening ideas.

The unstructured evening with live music #

This is the evening the engineers asked for, when nobody has a session to run or a slide to show. Book a jazz duo or a solo pianist for dinner, and everyone can talk easily over the music. It’s one of the most affordable ideas on this list, and it leaves plenty of time for the conversations your team flew in to have.

The founder's fireside with live music #

Many remote companies hold one evening where the founder or the CEO talks without slides. As with an award night, you’ll want music before and after, and quiet while they speak. A solo Spanish guitarist or the acoustic duo can play while everyone gathers, we can set up a microphone for the remarks, and then the music can pick back up. Learn more about how music works around remarks on our galas and award nights page.

Daytime team building at a Colorado offsite #

Your venue or a local outfitter can usually handle the daytime team building, whether that’s ropes courses, fly fishing, a cattle-roping clinic, a hike or a cooking class, just to name a few. We’d love to help with the evenings, and live music is an easy way to keep them relaxed after a full day.

If you’d like something for the agenda itself, Firstwater is a seventy-five-minute sound bath, with the whole team lying on the floor and nothing to do. It can open a morning or close an evening.
