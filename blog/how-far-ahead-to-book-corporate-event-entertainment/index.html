How far ahead to book entertainment for a company party

The short answer is whenever you're ready. The longer answer covers the order the decisions usually go in, the paperwork that can take longer than the booking, and what to have ready when you ask a band for a price.

Signet Artists · September 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways
  • You can book whenever you're ready. There's no date a company has to book its band by, and we're glad to help whether you ask early or late.
  • Planners usually make the decisions in this order: budget, venue, food and drink, entertainment, decor. Asking about entertainment while you're choosing the venue can save some email later, because the venue's rules decide what size of act will fit.
  • The paperwork can take longer than the booking. Vendor onboarding, a purchase order and a certificate of insurance can each take a while, so it helps to start them when you shortlist.
  • The date, the venue or a shortlist, a rough headcount and a sense of the program are enough for a band to suggest a size and give you a price.

The venue is booked, the date is on the calendar, and somebody in the office asks whether it’s too late to get a band. Usually it isn’t. Here’s how the entertainment decision tends to go for a company party in Denver, and what we’ll need from you to give you a price.

When do companies book the entertainment? #

It really varies from one company to the next. Some companies ask about the band the same week the venue is confirmed, and some ask a few weeks before the party, once the rest of the plan has settled. A band can often say yes much later than a ballroom can, which is why the entertainment can come last in the plan, and usually does. If the party is coming up sooner than you’d like, no worries. Just reach out and we can often make something great work.

A growing share of companies hold the holiday party in January instead, and several Denver venues now write their holiday packages to run from the middle of November into the third week of January. If you’re looking at January, check out our January office party ideas.

What order should you plan a corporate event in? #

When first-time planners ask on corporate event forums where to begin, most people tell them to start with the budget, then the venue, then food and drink, then activities or entertainment, and then decorations.

That order works well, and we’d change just one thing. We’d ask about the entertainment while you’re still choosing the venue, because its sound limit, its load-in hours (when the band can bring in gear) and its power decide the size of the act you can book there. A ballroom with a strict decibel limit and a restaurant with one outlet call for very different music. Our technical page lists what each size of act needs, and you’re welcome to forward it to a venue before you sign with it.

Galas, kickoffs and summer picnics #

The same order works for most other company events, too. A gala committee usually settles the venue and the honorees first, then comes to the music once the program is decided. Whoever plans a sales kickoff usually plans the evening along with the daytime program, and it’s often the same person who plans the holiday party. For a summer picnic, you’d pick the park or the brewery patio first, find out what cover and power it has, and look for the band after that.

How long does vendor paperwork take? #

Most companies need to get through vendor onboarding, a purchase order and a certificate of insurance. It’s all easy enough to get, but it can take time, which is why a booking you agreed on in a week can take a month to sign. We’d start the vendor setup as soon as you have a shortlist, and then the music is one thing you won’t have to chase later. Learn more about how we work with finance teams on our corporate page, and check out how a booking works for the steps in order.

What to have in hand when you ask for a number #

Have the date and the venue, or the two or three venues still in play. Add a rough headcount, and whether there’s a program and what’s in it: remarks, awards, a raffle, a video, an auction. Tell us whether you’d like to end with people dancing or with something quieter, and send the venue’s rules if you have them, like the decibel limit, the curfew and the load-in hours. With those, we can suggest the right size of act and give you a price for it. If you don’t have all of that yet, that’s fine. We can start with a range and work out the rest by email.
