How to make a corporate event sound like Colorado

Decide first whether the music is the event or the background. Then pick by the city you’re in. Here is how we’d do it in Denver, Boulder, Colorado Springs and the ski towns.

Signet Artists · November 13, 2026 · 4 min read

You’re bringing people to Colorado for a meeting, and somewhere in the brief it says the attendees should feel where they are. You can do some of that with the venue and some with the menu. You can do a lot of it with the music, if you choose knowing what each city sounds like.

To make a corporate event sound like Colorado, decide first whether the music is the event or the background. If it’s the event, hire the sound of the city you’re in. If it’s the background, hire songs people know.

Is the music the event, or the background? #

That’s the planner’s call. Both answers are fine, and one is safer.

If the night is meant to be immersive, we’d go authentic. Smart Meetings described a 2024 conference opening reception at the Colorado Convention Center where the hosts used local music, bison Bolognese and s’mores to make attendees feel like locals for a night. On a night like that, the guests came to take in the place.

If the music sits between conversations and the program, we’d go with nostalgia and familiarity. People are there to talk to each other, and they can talk over songs they know.

Corporate crowds lean toward the second. When people are somewhere for work, they care less about hearing the city, and what most of them want once the program ends is a clean, nostalgic party band.

Tejas Singh is a great safe choice: familiar songs, built up live with a looper. Roma Ransom, a violin and guitar duo, is the more artistic one.

What do attendees expect Colorado to sound like? #

John Denver, then jam bands, then bluegrass.

You can give them that. Destination Colorado, the state’s meeting-industry group, suggests bluegrass at a brewery tasting as a way to show off the state. Our Americana band plays that kind of evening as a trio or a quartet.

We’d skip the long jams. Those bands wander, and it’s easy to get lost in it when the evening has a schedule.

What does each city sound like? #

Denver’s own sound is neo-soul with a mountain sensibility, with funk and jazz all through it. It isn’t for everyone, and some groups will be happier with pop. What does Denver sound like? has the venues and the names. From our roster it’s the funk band, the soul and R&B band, the Changes Jazz Trio or Meghan Clarisse.

Boulder looks more like Burning Man, or Bali, and it has a slight aversion to the Americana the rest of the state loves. For a group that asks for something very Boulder, we pair a DJ with a live tabla player, and our Afro house DJ covers that. A sound bath is the quieter version. Chelsey Webb and Emily Barnes are the choice when the group would rather have songs.

Colorado Springs is split. One half is a developed jazz scene, fed by the horn players of the Air Force Academy Band, and our jazz duo plays the standards. The other half was raised on loud early-2000s rock. Corporate groups there usually want the party band: Quadrasonic or our millennial party rock band.

The ski towns have the most appreciative crowds a Front Range band ever plays for. The rest of the state is in What music is Colorado known for?

What should you check before you hire? #

If you’re hiring one person, make sure that person can carry a big sound and keep the energy up. There’s a difference between an amateur with a guitar and a real performer.

Then fit the music to the run of show. We wrote about how a live band works around a corporate program, and about other entertainment ideas for a Denver corporate event if music is only part of the plan. We also have pages for retreats, client dinners and holiday parties.

If you already have the venue, our pages on the Hyatt Regency at the convention center and Gaylord Rockies cover the spaces and the acts we’d suggest.
