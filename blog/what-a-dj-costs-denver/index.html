What a DJ costs in Denver

The word DJ covers two different purchases, and the market prices them a long way apart. Here's what a DJ costs for four hours, for a wedding and for a corporate party, and what you'd pay for a production DJ with lighting.

Signet Artists · September 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • In Denver, a DJ's four-hour block can run $350 to $700, and a DJ with production lighting can run $2,500 to $6,000. You're paying for two very different services, even though both are called a DJ.
  • Most US couples pay a wedding DJ $1,000 to $2,500, and the 2026 national average is $1,800. The premium tier runs $3,000 to $5,000.
  • A four-hour corporate party DJ can run $300 to $500, which may be the least expensive live entertainment for a company event.
  • A DJ's time can run $100 to $200 an hour, and the gear and extras change the price far more than the hours do.

If you're planning a party, the price of a DJ is probably the first number you look up. Couples compare a band to it, and companies compare a karaoke night to it. So it’s worth getting right, and the first thing to know is that the market publishes two very different prices for a DJ.

One is a DJ for a four-hour block, with a laptop, a controller, two speakers and somebody who can read the dance floor. The other is a production DJ, who brings a lighting rig, a wireless microphone for the toasts and a truck to carry it all, and who also MCs the program. Both are DJs. Their prices are far apart, and in our experience, most of the confusion over DJ quotes comes from comparing one with the other. All of the prices below are somebody else’s published numbers. We book DJs too, and we’re happy to recommend one for your event.

How much does a DJ cost for four hours? #

Four hours is the block most DJs quote, so it’s a good way to compare prices. You’re paying for the DJ’s time whether the dance floor is full or not. The market puts a DJ for four hours at a private event at $400 to $800, and a Denver DJ’s four-hour block at $350 to $700. Denver is slightly under the national price, which is typical of a market with plenty of DJs to choose from.

For a corporate party, the same four hours can run $300 to $500, because companies usually book a DJ without the wedding extras. That price may be the lowest of any live entertainment for a company event, and it’s why a DJ is the most common single booking for corporate parties.

How much does a wedding DJ cost? #

A wedding DJ costs more, and for a reason. The DJ runs the ceremony sound, the cocktail hour, the introductions, the first dance, the toasts and the dancing, and the package price reflects the whole day rather than four hours of it.

Most US couples pay $1,000 to $2,500, the 2026 national average is $1,800, and a wedding DJ for a four-hour block on its own runs $600 to $1,200. In a larger city the price is $1,600 to $2,200, and the premium tier, seasoned professionals in major metros with full-day coverage, runs $3,000 to $5,000. Denver falls between the four-hour price and the large-city price, depending on what’s in the package.

DJ prices, from a four-hour block to a production DJ #

Look at the two Denver prices, and you’ll see how big the jump is. It can be the same person either way, and the difference in price is paying for the lighting rig, the wireless microphone, the MC work and the truck that carries it all. When two DJ quotes come in far apart, the first thing to ask is which of these two services each DJ is quoting.

What affects the cost of a DJ? #

  • The gear. This includes speakers sized to the venue, a subwoofer for dancing, uplighting, dance-floor lighting and a wireless microphone for the toasts. Each one adds to the cost, and together they can make up the difference between the low and high prices above.
  • The role. A DJ who also MCs the program, runs the introductions and keeps things on time is doing a second job, and wedding packages include that in the price.
  • The hours. A DJ’s time runs $100 to $200 for each hour, and adding time on the day can run $200 to $400 for each extra hour across Colorado. In most cases, the hours affect the price less than the gear does.
  • The date. A holiday weekend in Denver can add 20 to 30 percent, and New Year’s Eve is a special case everywhere.

Should you book a DJ, a band or both? #

This is the comparison most people are actually making. A live wedding band averages $4,500 nationally against the DJ’s $1,800, and Denver bands run $2,500 to $8,000. Both can get people dancing, and a band also gives your guests something to watch. Want to compare the two? Check out our band or DJ guide.

A lot of hosts choose both, one after the other. Live musicians can play during arrival and dinner, and then the DJ can pick up from there through one sound system and on one contract, which can cost far less than booking the two separately. You can also have a saxophonist or a bass player perform on the dance floor along with the DJ, so there’s something to watch as well as dance to. Learn more about both options on our DJ page.
