What live music costs for a corporate event

A band's price has two parts. Here's what each part covers, how two hours compares with four, and what the total can come to for the most common Denver corporate events.

Signet Artists · August 5, 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways
  • Nationally, a cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000, and Denver bands run the same range, from a trio at the bottom to a full band with horns at the top.
  • Most acts charge for two things: a call-out fee that covers the event they commit to, no matter how long it runs, and a rate for the time they perform. That's why one hour of music rarely costs a quarter of what four hours costs.
  • Sound is the item that most often explains why two quotes at the same price can cover very different things. Ask whether it's included in the price.
  • Travel outside of Denver and a December weekend can each add a separate line to a quote. A holiday weekend in Denver can add 20 to 30 percent to the price.

You’ve got two band quotes, and they’re only a few hundred dollars apart. One includes sound and the other assumes the venue has it, travel is on one and missing from the other, and somebody upstairs wants a single price to approve.

That’s a pretty normal place to start a corporate music budget, so here’s what the market charges and what each price covers.

Why the price has two parts #

A call-out fee covers the whole event we commit to, however long it runs. That means local travel, load-in, setup, sound check, strike, load-out, and the sound equipment that comes along with all of it. An hourly rate then covers performance time.

We split it that way because of short bookings. A duo playing a one-hour cocktail set has the same drive, load-in and sound check as a four-hour reception, and the same strike after the guests leave. If a band priced purely by the hour, the one-hour booking would need a rate nobody would agree to, or the four-hour booking would have to subsidize it. Splitting the fee keeps both prices sensible, and it means you can check the math on your quote yourself.

What bands charge for corporate events #

A booking marketplace averages $1,150 for a Denver party band across listings running $230 to $3,550.

The other thing to think about is the size of your event. A small dinner and a big ballroom party need different bands, and in our experience, most corporate events need fewer musicians than the person booking them expects. The price depends far more on how many musicians are in the band and how many hours they play than on which act you choose.

How much does it cost to hire a band for two hours or four hours? #

A lot of planners ask what it costs to hire a band for an hour, for two hours or for four. Budgets are often written in hours, so here’s what the market charges when you look at it that way.

The first thing to know is that two hours doesn’t cost half of what four hours costs, because of the call-out fee. Only the playing time is cut in half, and playing time is the smaller part of the fee. A short booking is closer to two thirds of the price of a long one than to half, which is why the low end of every published range sits higher than the math suggests it should.

These prices depend on how long the music plays and how much sound equipment comes with it.

For a two-hour corporate booking, the best price to look at is the dinner price, since a two-hour booking is usually a seated dinner with a few speeches. Two musicians for two hours is the least expensive booking on these lists, and in our experience it’s also the one people most often overbuy, getting a quote for a quartet when a duo would have been perfect.

Going the other way, the Denver market charges $200 to $400 for each hour past the contracted set. It’s worth asking about that before you sign anything, because a four-hour booking that turns into five hours can break a budget.

Three common ways to set up live music for a corporate event #

A price range is easier to picture with a real event next to it. These three come up most often for corporate events in Denver, and here’s the size of band each one usually needs.

A client dinner. A jazz duo can play for three hours, staying under the conversation the whole time so nobody has to raise their voice across the table. A duo doesn’t need a stage, either.

A holiday party with a real dance floor. A quartet can play for four hours, keeping it quiet enough to talk through dinner and turning it up when it’s time to dance. Four musicians can be plenty for a ballroom dance floor, and it’s the horns and the second vocalist that push the price into show band territory.

A gala that runs the whole night. You might book a quartet for three hours, a duo for the cocktail hour before it and a DJ for the hour after. Covering all three with one booking can cost much less than making three separate bookings, because you’re paying for the drive, the load-in and the sound system once. Ask any act you’re comparing whether they price it that way.

Factors that affect price #

Six things can affect the price, and each one should be a separate line on the quote and on the contract. If they aren’t, ask for that before you compare the numbers.

  • Travel. On the Front Range, travel adds a small amount to the quote. It adds more along the I-70 corridor, which covers Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge and Winter Park. A booking in Aspen, Telluride, Steamboat or Crested Butte adds travel plus a night’s lodging, billed at what it actually costs.
  • Holiday and peak weekends. These weekends usually cost more than an ordinary weekend.
  • New Year’s Eve. This date is priced on its own just about everywhere.
  • Live-band karaoke. If you want your team to sing with the band behind them, that’s a separate line on the quote.
  • Learned songs. Ask whether the band can learn a song you choose, and how much notice it needs.
  • Overtime. Any time past the contracted set is billed by the hour.

What live music costs in December #

A holiday weekend in Denver usually comes with a 20 to 30 percent markup on the price of the music rather than on travel, so you can expect to pay more than the ranges above on a December Saturday.

What should live music cost for a company holiday party? #

Budget $2,500 to $8,000 for a cover or jazz band, which is what the national market charges for the kind of band most companies hire for a holiday party. That’s also the Denver range, from a trio at the bottom to a full band with horns at the top. Then adjust the number for your date, adding the markup above for a holiday weekend. For New Year’s Eve, just ask us.

If the party is a seated dinner rather than a dance floor, the band can be smaller and the price lower, with a jazz trio or acoustic duo for a dinner running $500 to $3,000. If it’s a full evening with dancing in a ballroom, the four-hour prices above are the range you’re in, and a show band with horns and a second vocalist can run $7,500 to $17,000.

What should a live music quote include? #

You can only compare two band quotes once you know what each one covers, and this is where quotes differ the most. Here’s what we can take care of for you:

  • Sound support that’s the right size for the venue
  • Load-in, setup, strike and load-out
  • A certificate of insurance
  • One itemized invoice, plus anything else your finance team needs
  • Timeline coordination before the event
  • Background music during breaks
  • A personal point of contact for the event

Sound is the first thing to check against another quote. A band that doesn’t bring its own sound system will either add one later or expect the venue to have one, and a venue’s house system may only be set up for speeches. Stage lighting and a dedicated engineer are optional add-ons, quoted as their own line when you want them.

Who approves the number

In many corporate bookings, the person choosing the band and the person approving the budget are two different people, and they’re rarely in the same meeting. Feel free to forward these market prices to whoever approves the budget, so nobody has to wait on a vendor to reply.
