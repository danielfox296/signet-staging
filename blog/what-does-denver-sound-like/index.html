What does Denver sound like?

Visitors expect John Denver. The city sounds more like neo-soul with a mountain accent, with funk and jazz all through it and a scene of songwriters next door. Here is where to hear it, and who we’d put in front of your guests.

Signet Artists · November 13, 2026 · 4 min read

You have people flying into Denver. A sales team, a board, a wedding party. Most of them have never been here, and they’ll land expecting John Denver. With that name, how could they not.

Denver sounds like neo-soul with a mountain sensibility behind it. Picture the words to “Jolene” sung over D’Angelo’s chords. Funk and jazz are all through it, the jam band crowd is enormous, and right next to all of that sits a scene of outlaw Americana and roots songwriters, the way Austin has one.

Where can a visitor hear it? #

We’d start at Meadowlark, a basement bar at 27th and Larimer. On Wednesday nights the guitarist Eman Alexander hosts Off the Cuff, which he started in 2023. The band improvises its way through neo-soul, R&B and funk, and this is the sound we mean.

We’d go back on a Monday for straight-ahead jazz. The Monday jam has run for well over ten years, and it’s where the best players in town go to blow off steam after their weekend shows. In 2015 members of Earth, Wind & Fire showed up to play while they were passing through on tour.

Downtown, at the performing arts complex, Dazzle hands its stage to the Blowing Loud Jam Sessions, hosted by the trumpeter Parris Fleming.

The bigger touring acts play Red Rocks, outside the city. Red Rocks is where Denver goes to hear everyone else.

Why funk and jazz? #

Both have been here a long time. Welton Street, in Five Points, was called the Harlem of the West. Duke Ellington, Louis Armstrong, Count Basie and Nat King Cole stayed at the Rossonian Hotel and played its lounge between their bigger Denver shows.

Three members of Earth, Wind & Fire went to East High School: Philip Bailey, Larry Dunn and Andrew Woolfolk. Eman Alexander started his Wednesday night at Meadowlark to honor that Five Points history.

Who are the songwriters? #

The second Denver is outlaw Americana and a roots sound close to Wilco. Its center is Clubhouse Recorders, a studio in Golden. The people making records there are after something plain and human, and the songwriting is what you notice first.

Thomas Owen is one of the best outlaw Americana songwriters in town. Meghan Clarisse won Folk/Americana Album of the Year at the 2026 Josie Music Awards for Shadows of a Ghost Town, and her shows put the singer out front. René Moffatt is a Texan, and he brought that with him. He’s the one pushing what a singer-songwriter can do with a voice. San Leandro takes Leo Albertoni’s songs and puts some of the city’s best funk and fusion players behind them.

This scene has a history too. The Lumineers played Meadowlark’s open-mic nights long before they played arenas.

What about the jam bands? #

They’re real, and the crowd for them is huge. The Grateful Dead and Phish have an enormous following here. Those bands wander, and at an event with a schedule it’s easy to get lost in it.

Is the real Denver right for your event? #

It isn’t for everyone. Some hosts want background music or pop, and that’s a fine thing to want.

If the music is meant to be the event, we’d give your guests the city. If it’s there between conversations, we’d give them songs they know. We go further into that choice in How to make a corporate event sound like Colorado.

Each has a page: the funk band, the soul and R&B band, the Changes Jazz Trio, the jazz duo, Chantil Dukart and Tejas Singh.
