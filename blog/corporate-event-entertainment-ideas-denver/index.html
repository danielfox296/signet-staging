Corporate event entertainment ideas in Denver

Here are eleven ideas for the evening part of a company event, music and otherwise, with what each one asks of your guests and how big it needs to be.

Signet Artists · September 14, 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways
  • Planners everywhere tend to say the same two things, that they don't want another casino night or another photo booth. We kept that in mind for every idea below.
  • The ideas that tend to go over best give your guests something to do together. The ones that just ask people to watch seem to be fading.
  • A cover or jazz band for a corporate event can run $2,500 to $8,000 in the Colorado market, and the price depends far more on how many musicians you book and for how many hours than on which act you choose.
  • After-parties are now a regular part of corporate events in Denver, whether that's after a conference at the convention center, a gala at one of the hotels next to it or a holiday party.

This job tends to land on someone’s desk the same way every time. Somebody senior says the event needs entertainment this year, and by the end of the week it’s up to whoever runs the office to find some. Then you search for ideas, and most of the lists you find are national and don’t have a date on them.

So here are our ideas for Denver in 2026, and where each one fits in your event. If price is your first question, start with our post on what live music costs for a corporate event. For a sense of what your own event might cost, take a look at our pricing page.

Start with what your corporate event is for #

A company event can be for one of four things, and the right entertainment depends on which one you’re planning. A reception is for people getting to know each other, so you’ll want music they can talk over. A dinner with a program is usually about honoring someone, so the musicians need to know when the remarks are coming. A celebration is about getting people dancing, so the band needs to hold back until it’s time to turn it up. A conference after-party is for a crowd from all over who’ve spent the day in a ballroom, so the act has to reach everyone at once.

Most company events are really two of these, one after the other.

Live band karaoke #

This is a great one if your office has already done the casino night. A real band plays hits from the 80s and 90s with its own singer, then hands the microphone to anyone who wants a song, with the words on a screen and a host out with your guests. The band can play plenty of songs on its own until somebody gets brave, and in our experience the first volunteers are usually a pair or a group.

The big difference from a karaoke machine is the band standing behind the singer. The band follows the singer’s tempo, one of the musicians can join in on the chorus, and the band can bring the song to a proper ending. It’s an excellent choice for a holiday party, a sales kickoff or a conference after-party, either on its own or as the high-energy part of a longer event. Learn more about how our live band karaoke works.

A DJ with a live musician #

This is the most common corporate booking we see, and it’s the easiest one to get approved. An open-format DJ, who plays all kinds of music, can read a mixed crowd right away, and adding a saxophonist or a bassist playing live along with the DJ gives your guests something to watch as well as dance to. That keeps things simple, with one line on your budget, one setup and music for your whole event.

The jazz trio that gives people a reason to stop and talk #

If you ask a group of planners why they’d bring a strolling magician to a company party, they’ll often say it gives guests a reason to stop and talk to each other. A jazz trio can do that for your whole event. The trio is guitar, upright bass and drums played with brushes instead of sticks, and they can keep the volume low enough for people to talk across a table. We love a trio for a reception or a dinner when the whole point is getting people talking.

A yacht rock band on the patio #

Yacht rock means the Doobie Brothers, Steely Dan, Hall and Oates, Toto and Christopher Cross, sung in harmony with a saxophone out front. It’s a great pick for a summer party, a client appreciation event on a patio or a later set once the awards are done. People in Denver have been searching for a yacht rock band for years, and many end up buying tickets to a touring one instead. This is the one to book if your crowd loves singing along to every chorus.

Colorado entertainment for out-of-town guests #

A group that’s in Denver for a conference or an incentive trip usually wants at least one evening that could only happen here, and a playlist in a ballroom sounds the same in every city. An Americana band with acoustic guitar, fiddle, upright bass and drums can play what people expect to hear in the mountains, like the Grateful Dead and Tom Petty, along with songs from Colorado artists like Nathaniel Rateliff and Gregory Alan Isakov. It’s a lovely choice for a welcome reception downtown, a ranch dinner or the last night of a trip to Vail.

A soul and R&B band for dancing after the awards #

Our soul and R&B band plays Stevie Wonder, Aretha Franklin, Al Green and the Motown songs everyone knows, with a singer out front and a horn on the big choruses if you’d like one. This is a great act for a ballroom event with a program before the dancing. The band can play quietly through dinner, stop for the remarks and the awards, and start the dancing as soon as the emcee is done. Take a look at our soul and R&B band on the Artist Roster, and learn more about how the music works around a program on our galas and award nights page.

A flamenco trio performance #

Sometimes you want one standout performance at your event. Dirty Flamenco is a flamenco trio that plays real palos (the traditional flamenco forms), and we can add a dancer if you’d like a floor show. We’ll help you find the right spot for it in your program. It’s perfect for a member event, an important client dinner or a themed party.

Solo piano through the reception #

Solo piano works well for the hour at the hotel bar before dinner, a small client dinner or an awards reception in a ballroom foyer. It’s one musician at the piano playing standards and covers, quietly enough for everyone to keep talking, and we can add a singer if you’d like vocals. It’s the smallest idea on this list, and it’s one that planners often ask for by name.

The comedian, the magician and the casino table #

A lot of lists lead with these three ideas, so here are a few notes on each. A comedian works best when the crowd is seated, sober enough to listen and shares the same references. That’s often true at a client dinner, and it’s rarely true at a holiday party once the bar opens. A strolling magician can work well at a reception because nobody has to do anything, and that’s also true of a trio, since the two do much the same job. Casino tables are what many planners are trying to get away from this year, so if you want one, we’d suggest keeping it to a side table.

We book live music, so these three aren’t something we offer.

Why choose live entertainment for a corporate event? #

People want something different from a company event now. Planners describe teams who spend their days on calls and their evenings on their phones, and if a party could have been a video, nobody really needed to be there. The events people talk about the next morning tend to be the ones where something happened live that a screen couldn’t replay.

That’s a big reason to choose a live act over a screen, a playlist or a booth. Planners are already saying so to each other, asking for something interactive and memorable rather than something people just watch.

What size of act you need for your headcount #

For fewer than about eighty guests, a duo or a solo musician can play your whole event from a corner of the venue. Between eighty and a hundred and fifty guests, a trio is the size to book, because drums played with brushes keep the music easy to talk over while filling twice the space a duo can. Above a hundred and fifty guests, you’ll want a quartet or more if you’re hoping for a full dance floor, and the band can keep the volume down through dinner before the dancing starts.

Two things can go wrong at a large event, whatever you book. A large group tends to split into lots of small conversations soon after arriving, so an idea that only reaches the people standing in line for it will miss most of your guest list. And a hotel’s house sound system is usually set up for speeches and may not be able to handle a band in a ballroom, so we’ll size our sound system to the venue.
