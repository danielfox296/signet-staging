Do you tip the band? Meals, drinks and tip jars

Nothing is expected, but there are a few house rules worth setting ahead of time. Here's how tips, tip jars, meals and drinks work for the band at your event, including the Colorado liquor rules that apply at a venue.

Daniel Fox, Signet Artists · November 27, 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • A tip is up to you. Nothing is expected.
  • A meal is welcome, and it isn't required. Tell the band ahead of time whether there is one.
  • At a licensed Colorado venue, every drink comes from the venue's bar. That includes the band's.

These are small questions. They tend to come up on the day, in a hallway, when you’d rather be doing something else. Settling them in advance takes one email.

Do you tip the band? #

A tip is up to you. Nothing is expected.

If you’d like to do something extra, the simplest way is cash in an envelope to the bandleader at the end of the night, who splits it with the band. A short note works too. So does asking your photographer to credit the musicians by name when the gallery goes out.

What about a tip jar? #

Some musicians put out a tip jar at public shows, where the audience is paying for the music directly. At a wedding or a company party, you’ve already paid. A tip jar on stage asks your guests to pay a second time.

It’s your call. Our acts only put out a tip jar when the client has said yes to it, so if you’d like one, just tell us ahead of time.

Should you feed the band? #

We don’t ask for meals. If you’re serving one, the band would love one, and a vendor meal is perfect.

What helps most is knowing in advance. A band playing three or four hours plans its breaks and its own food around the answer. If there’s a meal, say when it’ll be ready and where they can eat out of sight of the guests.

Can the band drink? #

At a venue, this is mostly decided by Colorado law.

Hotels, restaurants and event venues serve alcohol under a state liquor license, and the bartenders work under it. Colorado makes it unlawful to drink alcohol on licensed premises that wasn’t bought there (C.R.S. 44-3-901). So a cooler of the band’s own beer doesn’t belong at a hotel. The same law makes it unlawful to serve anyone who is visibly intoxicated, and that covers the band too.

So drinks for the band go through the venue’s bar. If you’re hosting the bar and you want the band included, tell the venue’s catering contact whether their drinks go on your tab. At a party in your own home, it’s your call.

Our performers don’t drink at your event unless you offer. If they accept, they keep it to a minimum and stay professional all night.
