Colorado favorites

An Americana band for hire in Denver for welcome receptions, retreats and weddings.

You’ve got people flying in for your event, and you want it to sound like Colorado from the first song. This is the band to book when you want that sound. It’s a fiddle or mandolin and an upright bass behind a singer on acoustic guitar, playing the songs people expect to hear in the mountains alongside songs that were written right here.

It’s a great fit for a conference welcome reception downtown, a ranch or resort dinner in the mountains, a company picnic or a patio wedding.

What does an Americana band play?

The band plays the songs people love in the mountains, like Friend of the Devil by the Grateful Dead and Harvest Moon by Neil Young, along with John Prine, Johnny Cash, Dolly Parton, Tyler Childers and Chris Stapleton. It also plays music from right here in Colorado, including Ho Hey by the Lumineers from Denver.

Have a song in mind that isn’t here? Just let us know when you book, and the band can usually learn it. If you’d rather book a Colorado singer-songwriter by name, take a look at Meghan Clarisse.

Sizes

A trio, or a quartet with drums.

The trio
The trio is a lovely size for a welcome reception, or for a dinner where people want to talk.
The quartet
Add drums to the trio and you’ve got a dance band. It’s a great way to get people moving to Tom Petty and the Grateful Dead at a ranch dinner, a company picnic or the last night of a retreat.

Americana band questions

Have a question we haven’t covered here? Take a look at our full FAQ, or just reach out. Wondering what Americana is, or how it differs from country and bluegrass? Learn more about Americana music. We also wrote about what music Colorado is known for.

Is it a bluegrass band?
It can be when you want it to be. The fiddle or mandolin can take the lead on bluegrass standards, and the song list goes well beyond that, with classic rock and Colorado favorites, too. If you’d like more bluegrass or less of it, we can choose the songs with you.
How loud is it?
The trio can keep it low enough for easy conversation at a reception or a dinner, and outdoors, we can set up the sound so the back of the lawn can still hear the fiddle. The quartet has drums, so it’s louder. Mountain venues often have a sound limit or a curfew, so if yours does, just let us know and we’ll plan around it.
Can it play outdoors and at altitude?
Yes, and lawns, ranches and resort decks are some of the best places to hear this band. The band will need a dry, level spot under cover with power nearby, and we’ll work out a weather plan with you ahead of time.
Can we add time or ask for a song?
Yes. Let us know the song when you book and the band can usually learn it. If you think your event might run long, let us know that, too.
