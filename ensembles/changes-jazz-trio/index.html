Changes Jazz Trio

Doug Emery, guitar. Sri Viswanath, fretless bass. Brian Jaffe, drums.

You know that moment in a great little jazz club when a song you love goes somewhere you didn’t expect? Changes brings that to your event. They’re a Denver trio on guitar, bass and drums, playing jazz standards with a modern edge, and a saxophone can join them when you want the music front and center for a while.

Trusted and booked by

The trio keeps its arrangements relaxed and easy to listen to, and the solos can get a little adventurous. They can stay in the background through a dinner, and they’re just as happy to play for a crowd that’s there to listen.

Doug Emery studied guitar performance at Berklee, played more than three thousand shows across New England, from jazz duos to wedding bands, and now teaches music full time in Denver. Sri Viswanath spent decades playing bass in New York, on stages from the jazz club Smalls to the Plaza Hotel, and performed on Radha Thomas’s 2019 tribute to Chet Baker. Brian Jaffe studied at the Lamont School of Music at the University of Denver, and he’s played more than a hundred theater productions in Denver pit orchestras since 2011.

Occasions

Perfect booking for

Client dinners
The trio can play through a client dinner with the drums kept soft, so you can still talk business. If there’s a program, add a saxophone and the quartet can play a featured set. Need music for the rest of your company event? We can help with that, too.
Wedding cocktail hours
If you love jazz and want your cocktail hour to feel a little more modern, this trio is an excellent choice.
Galas and award nights
The trio is a great choice for the time before your program starts. Book the quartet with saxophone when you want the music to be a real part of your gala, and it can stop on your emcee’s cue.
Dinner parties and milestone birthdays
If you’re hosting a dinner party or a milestone birthday and you love jazz, this is the trio to call. The trio fits a home or a patio.

Changes Jazz Trio questions

Got a question that isn’t here? You may find the answer on our full question list. Wondering what makes a jazz trio? Learn more about the classic lineups.

What does Changes Jazz Trio play?
They play instrumental jazz standards, so there’s no singer.
How is this different from the jazz duo?
Our jazz duo is guitar and upright bass playing standards for cocktails and dinner, and we pick the musicians to suit your date. Changes is a trio with drums and a fretless bass, so it has a more modern sound, and you’ll know who the three musicians are before you book.
How loud is it?
The trio can play cocktails and dinner at a volume where everyone can talk easily. The quartet gets louder for a featured set with the saxophone, since that’s the music you want people to stop and hear. If your venue has a sound limit, let us know the number and we’ll stay under it.
Where is the trio based?
Denver. All three musicians live and work in the Denver area, and the trio plays all over the Front Range and in the mountain towns.
Can Changes play outdoors?
Yes, as long as there’s a covered spot out of the weather. We’ll work out a backup plan with you ahead of time, since Colorado weather can turn fast.
